# The FOMC overlay's gate — written 2026-09-15, before the September 16 outcome

The paper book runs a provisional rule: three sessions before an FOMC
decision, if SPY's trailing five-session return is negative, held shares
are cut to half at the next open, held through the decision session and
restored at the open after. Its evidence is two completed meetings since
the guidance change and a 2021–2026 simulation in which every variant
cost return (`fomc-window-evaluation-2026-09-13.md`). That is not enough
to keep it or to retire it. This gate is registered now so the decision
is made by a rule written before the outcomes, not after.

## What is measured

For each meeting from 2026-09-16, the paper account's own fills and
closes give an exact counterfactual: the book that never traded the
overlay holds every share the overlay sold until the overlay buys it
back. On any session the two books differ by the sum over confirmed
fills of shares × (close − fill price), signed. Per meeting the block
records, over the window from the session before the first cut to the
restoration session:

- **effect**: live equity minus the no-overlay equity at the end of the
  window, in dollars and as a share of equity at the window's start;
- **effect after costs**: the same less 25 basis points of the overlay's
  traded notional, since the paper account pays no commission;
- **drawdown**: the worst peak-to-trough of each path inside the window.

Code: `backend/market/fomc_gate.py`; written nightly to
`data/market/desk/fomc-gate.json`; tests in `backend/tests/test_fomc_gate.py`.

## The rule

After **six completed meetings** starting with 2026-09-16 (through about
mid-2027):

- **keep** the overlay when the summed effect after costs is positive
  and the live drawdown is not deeper than the no-overlay drawdown in
  more than half the meetings;
- **retire** it otherwise.

Until six meetings are complete the standing is **waiting**, whatever
the running total says. A meeting where the rule did not fire (SPY not
weak) contributes nothing and does not count. Any change to the
policy's parameters, trigger or window restarts the count at zero. No
parameter is tuned on these meetings; a different rule is a new gate.

## What this does not claim

Six meetings is the smallest sample the operator accepts for a decision,
not a statistical guarantee. The counterfactual is exact for the paper
account's fills but says nothing about market impact at real size. The
2021–2026 simulation remains the only long-history evidence, and it is
against the overlay.
