The plan on the feature branch is good. ## 5. Where ML, DL and RL can earn a place, and where they cannot

Your objective has four distinct subproblems, and they have very different odds. The honest ordering, from the repo's own evidence plus what the literature says about long-only unlevered books:

**A. Selection and grading (daily): learnable, small edge, best label density.** The grade should become a learned ranker whose target is what the book actually earns: next-open to +20-session-open return relative to the equal-weight book (`learned_policy.relative_open_labels`, `:119`, is the right label; the sweep's beta-neutral close-to-close IC was the wrong objective). Model: gradient-boosted trees with a top-K objective (LambdaRank or decile classification), trained on the point-in-time 500+ name universe, not the 94 survivors, with the current analyst stances and convictions as features plus the 31 causal `alpha.alpha_features`. Meta-labelling on top: the rule proposes, the model sizes the bet (the "smoothing was the whole edge" finding in `opinions.py:24-38` is exactly what meta-labelling formalises). Expected gross gain 0-2 CAGR points. The one place deep learning has already paid is text: extending the fundamentals-forecasting LightGBM with release/filing text (embeddings under strict acceptance-time cutoffs, G2 fixed first) has real label density (quarterly, thousands of filings across 532 names) and low reflexivity.

**B. Day-type / regime ("be in volatile names on green days, out before red days"): the hardest problem here, and the repo's own learned brake and ridge gate both lost CAGR.** Predicting next-session direction of SPY is close to hopeless with free data. What is somewhat predictable is the *distribution*: next-session realised volatility and dispersion of the volatile basket, and the probability of a large down move in SMH/IGV/EW-book conditional on how today's daily candle was built from its 15-minute bars (close location in range, last-hour return, range expansion, overnight gap), VIX term structure (VIX/VIX3M, free), breadth, and the event calendar. Frame it as a calibrated probabilistic classifier, P(basket return < −x over 1-5 sessions), scored by Brier skill against climatology on 2016-2023 walk-forward, purged. Its only permitted action is an exposure scalar in [0,1] and the SPY-vs-cash destination choice, and it must fit inside your 1.5 CAGR insurance budget. Kill criterion, written now: Brier skill below 0.02 out of sample, or cost above budget → keep the frozen brake, ship the model as a banner only.

**C. 15-minute dip entries and extension trims: not an alpha source, an execution and position-management layer.** Standalone intraday strategies were negative at 3 bp; confirmation entries lost 0.5-2% per episode; the measurable bp are in execution (sells at the close +24 bp, t 2.8; first-hour buys −37 bp). So the learned 15-minute models should answer conditional questions on positions the daily layer already decided: given a decided buy today, will the session's VWAP sit below the open (wait for the dip) or above (go now)? Given a held name up X% intraday with a rejection bar, is P(next-5-session return < 0) high enough to trim? Given a volume-confirmed break of a level, does selling half now beat selling at the close? These are meta-labels with hundreds to thousands of events a year on 94 names, learnable with gradient boosting on the causal feature inventory that already exists (`intraday_entry._walk`, `timing_research.readings`, `market_intraday_rl._features`, `intraday_replay.execution_proxy`), and a small sequence model (GRU/TCN over the developing day plus the prior five sessions) as the challenger to the hand-coded ACCEPT/FAILED_HIGH/BREAK thresholds. The sequence model's job is to *label structure*, not to predict returns. Judge every rule per order in bp against the incumbent's clock with a paired test, at your registered bar (20 bp, t > 3), never by CAGR.

**D. Portfolio balancing (weights): do not use RL.** With ~65 independent 20-session periods since 2016, allocation RL cannot be distinguished from equal weight, and your own CEM/AWR runs showed exactly that. The balancer should be a constrained optimiser with learned inputs: expected relative return from A, exposure scalar from B, and a turnover penalty, subject to fixed operator constraints (20% hold limit, no leverage, cooldowns, daily action cap). Capped equal weight over the top-K names with a rank buffer is the baseline it must beat. RL is appropriate in exactly one place in this repo: execution scheduling within the day, where you have 82k order-sessions and the direct-policy already found +2.4 bp on buys.

**"Nothing hard-coded" needs a precise meaning, or it contradicts your own gate discipline.** Read it as: no literal in decision code; every threshold, lookback, cap and weight is either an operator constraint in versioned config or a fitted parameter produced by a documented walk-forward procedure on the choosing window, stored in a provenance-hashed bundle (the `opportunity_neural_v1.npz` + `identity()` pattern already does this). The frozen numbers in G9 should be re-derived by that procedure and frozen as fitted values, which is different from being chosen by looking at the 2018-2026 curve.
