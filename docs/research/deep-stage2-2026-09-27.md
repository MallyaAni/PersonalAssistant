## Reading it

1. **The models learn structure now.** With sixty sessions, market context
   and the desk's evidence as inputs, the 20-session drawdown forecast has
   an IC of 0.09-0.15 at t 5-9 - a strong, stable signal in both windows -
   and coming downgrades are detected at AUC 0.58-0.63. The operator's
   point stands: inputs made the difference; stage 1's five sessions of
   bars alone could not see this.
2. **What they learn is not tradable on this book.** Dropping the names the
   model says will draw down costs 1.6-2.3 bp a session (t -2.5 to -3.9):
   the names most likely to fall are the ones earning the return, and the
   grade rotation already removes the ones whose structure actually
   breaks. The downgrade forecast's decision test is the one that comes
   closest (+4.2 bp/s on 2024-2026, t 1.9 for the CNN) and it is zero on
   the choosing window, so it fails the floor honestly.
3. **Pretraining helped the representation and not the decision.** The
   masked-patch pretrained encoder gives the best drawdown IC (0.153) and
   the worst recent-window rank IC (-0.022): a better model of the series
   is not a better model of what to do.
4. **Return forecasting on 15-minute bars is unchanged at IC ~0.01.** Three
   architectures, two input designs, walk-forward, no search: the
   name-specific next-session return signal in these bars is about a
   hundredth of a rank correlation, and the literature's "accuracy is not
   a signal" warning holds at this horizon and cost.
5. **Where the skill can still pay.** The drawdown and downgrade forecasts
   are good inputs for questions the book has not asked yet: which *new*
   entry to take first when cash is short (the redeploy leg funds pro
   rata today), and whether a dip in a graded name is one the forecast
   expects to recover - a conditional dip rule, registered next. They are
   not inputs for selling.
