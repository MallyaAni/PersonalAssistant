# Risk dial and core-satellite: the owner's choice, measured — 2026-10-28

**Status: measurement for the P4 owner decision. No live change.** The
installed rule is run with only its volatility target changed, on the same
frozen point-in-time cache that produced the program's honest baseline, so
the comparison is like for like. The owner chooses the setting; the
release ceremony installs it.

## Method

`policy_book.simulate` with `Rule(target=...)`; every other parameter is
the installed rule (A entry / B hold, 1% minimum trade, leader tilt,
25% name cap, 0.5 exposure floor, next-open fills). Two honest universes:
the S&P 500 point-in-time membership, and its Information Technology and
Communication Services subset. Costs at 10 and 25 bp. The choosing leg
runs from the cache's first sizable session (2015-04-07; the desk's
grades are effective from 2016) through 2023-12-29; the reported leg is
2024-01-02 through 2026-10-23. The cache is
`~/dsv41/pit2/cache.npz`, the runner `~/dsv41/pit2/risk_dial.py`, the
payload `~/dsv41/pit2/risk_dial.json`.

The 0.25 target at 10 bp reproduces the program's baseline exactly:
S&P 500 PIT 14.8% CAGR, −34.7% worst drawdown, Sharpe 0.90; IT+Comm PIT
19.6%, −35.7%, 0.96.

## What the dial says

On the broad universe the dial is nearly flat: 14.2%, 14.8% and 15.0% at
10 bp, with worst drawdowns of −35.1%, −34.7% and −36.0%. The book is
already 95-97% invested, so the volatility brake rarely binds; changing
its target mostly changes the cash at the margin. The 0.30 setting buys
0.2 CAGR points for 1.3 deeper drawdown points and no Sharpe gain; 0.20
gives up 0.6 CAGR points for 0.4 deeper drawdown. Neither is a meaningful
improvement on 0.25.

On the thematic universe the dial is monotonic and worth choosing:
0.20 gives up 1.2 CAGR points (18.4% against 19.6%) and buys 4.7 points
of worst drawdown (−31.0% against −35.7%), with the best Sharpe of the
three (0.98). 0.30 adds 0.2 CAGR points for 3.3 deeper drawdown points and
a lower Sharpe (0.93). **0.25 is the knee; 0.20 is the cautious setting;
0.30 is not worth it.** At 25 bp the ordering is the same: 0.20 16.3% /
−31.8%, 0.25 17.4% / −36.7%, 0.30 17.7% / −40.0%.

Each ten points moved from the theme to SPY costs about 0.5-0.6 CAGR
points and cuts about one drawdown point; the Sharpe is flat (0.96 to
0.94). The blend is a risk-level choice, not an efficiency gain. The
smallest blend that materially changes the drawdown is 80/20: −2.2
drawdown points for −1.1 CAGR points.

## Recommendation

- **Keep the installed 0.25 target.** It is the knee on the thematic
  universe and the broad universe is insensitive to the dial.
- If the owner wants the cautious setting, **0.20 on the thematic book**
  is the meaningful choice: the best Sharpe and 4.7 fewer drawdown
  points for 1.2 CAGR points. Do not use 0.30.
- If the owner wants a core-satellite account, **80/20 theme/SPY** is the
  efficient first step; deeper blends trade return for risk roughly
  linearly.

## Caveats

- The cache is frozen at 2026-10-24: grades, band, tilt and membership
  are the desk as it stood then. Only `Rule.target` changes, so the
  comparison across settings is clean, but the absolute levels are not a
  fresh backtest of today's desk.
- The store holds bars only for names it carries today; the membership is
  dated but the price history is survivor-biased. The absolute levels are
  flattered; the comparison across settings is the point.
- The core-satellite blend is arithmetic on the rule's equity and SPY's
  adjusted close, rebalanced daily. It does not model rebalancing costs,
  taxes, or costs on the index leg; the rule leg is 10 bp. Treat the
  blend's CAGR as a slight overstatement.
- The choosing leg starts at the cache's first sizable session
  (2015-04-07), not 2016-01-01. The desk's grades are effective from
  2016, so the first months are mostly cash; the program's 2016-2023
  label is approximate.
