## What the decomposition says

Read down the graded lines on 2016-2023 at 25 bp:

- Equal weight of every member the book could have held: **27.7%**, ahead
  of QQQ by 9 points, in 20 of 20 offsets, t 2.7. The sector book carries
  a real edge without any hindsight name.
- Keep only the names the desk grades A or A+, equal weight, fully
  invested: **29.9%**. The grade adds about two points; ahead of equal
  weight in 19 of 20 offsets, t 1.5 (PSR 0.92). A modest, positive,
  not-yet-significant selection signal. This is the first time the grade
  has been shown to select rather than merely to concentrate.
- The same under the operator's 20% hold limit: **28.3%**, level with
  equal weight, because with few A names the cap leaves cash idle.
- The same with a 10% cap (the plan's P1.2 as written): **17.8%**. The
  cap costs twelve points: it is idle cash, not selection.
- The frozen rule: **14.4%**. Inverse-volatility weights, the 0.30
  volatility target, the regime multipliers, the top-decile concentration
  (two to four names on the early book) and the band entries together cost
  a further three to four points, and 15 points against the graded book
  fully invested.

So the operator's instinct that the grading system was the one reliable
piece is borne out; the sizing layer built on top of it is what loses,
and it loses because it holds too little of the book. The learned ranker
on price features is worse than everything (12.6%, t -3.2 against equal
weight): concentrated in ten momentum names, it buys what has already run.
The same ranker on the desk's own evidence, the alpha summaries and the
regime context does better (17.0%) - the features matter, as expected -
but a ten-name book chosen by any forecast still trails holding every
member, and on the hindsight book the very same model shows 140% a year
for 2024-2026, which is the cleanest demonstration in this repository of
what a learned model "finds" on a survivor universe. The rotation fix does
nothing (13.4% against 14.4%).

## Recommendation

1. The release-train candidate `/4` is `ew_graded_20`: every A/A+ member
   of the point-in-time book at equal weight, capped at 20%, fully invested
   when ten or more names qualify, spare cash in cash, no volatility
   target and no regime multiplier. It beats the incumbent by about 14
   CAGR points on the choosing window in every offset, at both costs, and
   it is the simplest rule the desk has ever run. Its drawdown is deeper
   (-43% against -25% for the incumbent over the whole window, -38% for
   equal weight) because it is fully invested; the operator's stated
   objective is total return with drawdown reported, not gated.
2. It goes to the 4-6 week fidelity shadow next, not to the paper account:
   the gate's forward step is fill fidelity, and the paper executor's
   band entries, green-day skip and close-vs-open conventions all have to
   be re-derived for a fully invested equal-weight book.
3. Selection work stops here for now. The grade's own selection is worth
   about two points and is not yet significant; the learned rankers are
   worse. The next measurable money is in deployment (done), then
   execution and the day-type question (below), then the 15-minute layer.

**Verdict: INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE.** No model beats the trailing base rate
on the choosing window (best skill -0.001; every five-session model is
clearly worse than climatology), and the exposure diagnostic gives up
three to six CAGR points for three to four points of drawdown - the same
shape as every brake this desk has measured. The small positive skill in
2024-2026 is reported and not acted on. With the regime-relative label the
question was "can tomorrow's bad day be seen beyond what today's
volatility already says", and the answer on this data is no. The day-type
idea stays a banner at most; the risk-off destination question (cash or
SPY on a broad break) remains a long-history study of the frozen brake,
not a learned one.

## Caveats that stay attached to every number above

Exited names are unpriced in the store (the membership file lists them;
the panel holds today's tickers), so equal weight of the point-in-time book
still omits acquired and delisted members - mostly names bought out at a
premium, so the omission is unlikely to flatter. Sub-industry history is
not tracked, and "semiconductors plus software" is itself a 2026 choice:
the names are point in time, the sector call is not. Analyst ranks are
computed on the full 94-name panel and then restricted, so a 2018 grade is
a rank against names the book did not hold then (contemporaneous data,
not future data). The paired t at lag 20 on about 2,000 sessions is honest
about overlap but not about the 20-offset multiplicity, which the "offsets
above" column carries instead.
