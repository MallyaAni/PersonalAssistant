## Reading it

1. **A level does not, on average, separate dips that hold from dips that
   continue.** The operator's hypothesis, as registered, fails on the one
   cell that tests it. On 2016-2023 the level families sit within about
   ±6 bp of their controls, with no t above 1.7. The exception is VWAP,
   which goes the wrong way (point 3).

2. **What does separate is the isolated level.**
   - A support touch with exactly one level family in its zone closes
     15.8 bp better than its matched controls (t 6.3). It bounces 5.4
     points more often and breaks 2.2 points less often. The same cell on
     the reported window gives +13.7 bp (t 3.5). Resistance mirrors it:
     −13.6 bp (t −6.5), turned back 6.4 points more often.
   - Zones of three or more families do nothing or the opposite (−3.6 bp
     and −8.0 bp to the close).
   - This reverses the plan's expectation that confluence strengthens a
     level. The plan's own caveat explains it. The dense zones are VWAP,
     the opening range and the prior day's levels, which sit near the
     price by construction. An isolated level is usually a daily level in
     open space (a swing, an average, the prior week) that the price
     reached on its own.
   - **The bounce does not carry.** Isolated support touches give back
     −1.9 bp the next day and −14 bp over five sessions on 2016-2023, and
     −16.5 bp (t −2.6) the next day on 2024-2026. It is an intraday
     reversion at the level, not a turn.

3. **VWAP is not support; it is a momentum pivot.**
   - Price that comes down to the session VWAP from above keeps falling
     into the close: −12.5 bp against controls (t −4.0), and −16.7 bp
     (t −3.0) on 2024-2026.
   - Price that rises to it from below keeps rising: +8.9 bp (t 2.3) and
     +17.9 bp (t 2.7).
   - Crossing the VWAP continues. That is the day-trader's "above VWAP is
     bullish", measured on this book at fifteen-minute resolution.

4. **None of it moves the book as a fill rule.** Filling at the first
   level zone below the open fills 73% of orders at a level, against 41%
   for the 1% dip. It prices them 5-7 bp better per fill on 2016-2023 and
   16-18 bp worse on 2024-2026. The session difference is zero either way.
   It is the same arithmetic as every timing study here: at 0.38 orders a
   session and 5-9% of NAV each, a 15 bp per-fill edge is worth about
   0.4 bp a session.

5. **What follows.**
