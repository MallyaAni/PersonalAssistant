# Stage 4: grade-conditional multi-day timing, trained on the turn itself: pre-registration (2026-09-29)

## What the operator asked

**His points (2026-09-29).**

- "A grade A will likely have buys more often on dips and lower grades
  might have sell more often ... grade gated or weighted."
- "We should be able to detect structural turnaround when we are buying or
  selling. It sometimes takes many many days to happen because of trend
  following and is very complicated over different timeframes."
- "Couldn't CNNs detect structure shifts?" Mean reversion, Bollinger
  squeezes, and "those strategies must be researched by you."
- **His question:** "are you sure you used ML to try and predict the right
  thing?"

**The answer is no.** Every model on this book was trained on one of
these:

- the next session's rank or variance (stage 1);
- a 20-session rank, downgrade or drawdown (stage 2);
- "this 15-minute bar or today's close" (stage 3, T-I);
- a 20-session relative return used to pick names (stage 3, T-S1).

None was trained on the question he asks: *the grade says buy (or sell)
this name, so is now the moment, or will the price or the structure turn
in our favour over the next few sessions?* The triple-barrier target that
asks it (research note T-B1) was proposed and never built. Stage 3's
answer ("nothing beats the 1% dip, nothing beats holding the whole graded
book") says nothing about multi-day timing.

This note fixes, before any stage-4 code exists, a test of exactly that:

- the decision and the control;
- the candidate rules from the research and the operator's list;
- three model families trained on the timing target itself: trees, the
  Jiang-Kelly-Xiu chart CNN, and the stage-3 sequence model;
- the rows, labels, walk-forward, decision test, kill criteria and trial
  count.

Nothing below is tuned after the run. Any change is a new, counted trial.

## What is known before this note (disclosed)

**Prior tests on this book.** The survey was made for this note; the
details are in the notes cited.

- **Waiting has been tried only in simple forms, and it lost.**
  - A two-session wait for the low to hold after a dip "gave it all back"
    (`cli/market_dipadd.py`, old book).
  - Every intraday confirmation lost to buying at the open:
    −0.51% to −2.01% per episode (intraday-timing-2026-09-15).
  - EMA pullback entries and a structure exit lost on ten A+ picks
    (pick-timing-audit-2026-09-13).
- **Structure has tested weakly.**
  - The higher-high/higher-low state carried no forward return
    (`cli/market_structure.py`, |t| ≤ 1.4).
  - Stretched quality names did not snap back (`cli/market_snapback.py`).
- **Same-session and 20-session timing are recorded.** Same-session timing
  of the `/4` reset orders is flat: the seven fill conventions land within
  ±0.2 bp a session (execution-timing-2026-09-27). The ML fill models are
  at or below zero (stage 3, T-I). Mid-cycle trims and dip-adds are all
  RECORD (profit-taking-2026-09-27).
- **The gaps.** No test waited several sessions for a turn before a `/4`
  buy, conditioned timing on the grade, or trained any model on a turn
  target.

**The literature.** Two memos were written for this note:

- [stage4-lit-structure-2026-09-29.md](stage4-lit-structure-2026-09-29.md):
  market structure, turning points, regime detection, pullbacks and falling
  knives, CNNs and sequence models, multi-timeframe confirmation;
- [stage4-lit-reversion-2026-09-29.md](stage4-lit-reversion-2026-09-29.md):
  short-term reversal, RSI(2), IBS and band reversion, squeezes and
  breakouts, grade- and analyst-conditioned timing, exits.

Their shared reading:

- **Reversal is dead in large caps.** Plain short-term reversal earned
  0.00% a month in 2010-2026 and was negative in large caps (Fama-French
  data, the memo's Appendix A).
- **What survives** is conditional: it pays in stress, after no-news moves,
  and industry-relative.
- **Direction.** Squeezes forecast volatility, not direction. Stops and
  delayed exits mostly cost.
- **Priors.** The memos put about 3-8% on any multi-day timing rule adding
  +2 bp a session to this book.

**A path diagnostic, run before this note.** It is descriptive only, and
no candidate was priced: `scorecards/stage4/path_diagnostic.py`, output
`path_diagnostic.json`. It reads the daily paths, from the next open, after
a name enters A/A+ (560 events), leaves A/A+ (570) and on every A/A+ day
(16,553):

| Event | Price change over 5 / 10 sessions, bp (t) | A better close within 5 sessions | A move of 1 daily sd within 5 | Best close within 5 (oracle), bp |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Enters A/A+ (buy) | +75 (+2.7) / +142 (+4.6) | 72% lower | 40% down | 233 |
| Leaves A/A+ (sell) | +45 (+1.9) / +116 (+3.7) | 81% higher | 48% up | 290 |
| Any A/A+ day (a reset top-up) | +63 (+5.3) / +130 (+7.3) | 73% lower | 41% down | 245 |

- **Market stress.** When the VIX is 25 or more, upgraded names rise +300
  bp in 5 sessions (t +3.3), and downgraded names rebound +155 bp (t
  +3.3). Under 25 the changes are +34 (t 1.5) and +25 (t 1.0).
- **Grades.** A+ and A behave alike.
- **The highest close after a downgrade comes late,** at a median day 13-14
  of 20. That is the upward drift. After an upgrade the lowest close comes
  at a median day 8.

These readings shaped two choices:

- the window of five sessions;
- D1's split by side: in stress, buys act at once and sells wait.

D1 is read with that in mind. The readings are disclosed here and
accounted for below.

What they say plainly:

- **The grade's buys are right to act at once on average.** Upgraded and
  A/A+ names rise +63 to +75 bp in five sessions, and +300 in stress.
- **Every name in this book drifts up.** A downgraded name still rose +45
  bp over the next five sessions, less than an A/A+ name's +63. In stress
  it rebounded +155. An immediate sell gives up that drift, so waiting to
  sell gains mostly because it holds the market longer. That is exposure,
  not timing, and criterion 6 below separates the two.
- **A better price inside five sessions is common.** It comes three to
  four times in five.
- **A perfect timer would save about 230-290 bp an order.** A model must
  tell, before the fact, which orders will get that better price.

## The decision

**Which orders.** Every buy and sell the live executor makes, as the
`ew-redeploy` control runs it (`profit_taking.control_options`, the
executor under `mc-redeploy`, the operator's live choice):

- reset buys, top-ups and trims;
- idle-cash redeploy buys;
- mid-cycle band entries and deferred retries;
- rotation (downgrade) exits.

**The control.** The board's rule, `dip_or_close`, for every order:

- a buy fills at the first 15-minute close in session t+1 at or below the
  open × 0.99, else at the official close;
- a sell mirrors it at open × 1.01.

This is the stage-3 T-I control (`fill_timing`).

**What a candidate may do.** It may make an order **wait up to W = 5
sessions** (t+1..t+5) for its trigger. If the trigger never comes, the
order fills at the official close of session t+5. Nothing is skipped and
no order is ever resized.

- **While a buy waits, its money is cash.** That is what the account would
  hold.
- **While a sell waits, the shares are held.**

The per-order gain against the control is the log price ratio, which is
the exact value ratio at any common later date:

    buy:  g = 10^4 · ln( P_control / P_candidate )     [bp of the order]
    sell: g = 10^4 · ln( P_candidate / P_control )

The book's gain on a session is the sum, over the orders decided that
session, of the order's weight times g. The weight is its notional over
the account's equity at the decision. Both lines trade the same orders
once each, so costs cancel.

## The candidates

σ is the name's standard deviation of the last 20 daily log close-to-close
returns through t. C is the close, and H and L are the day's high and low.
Fills "at a bar" use the SIP 15-minute cube: the first bar k = 0..23
(closing 09:45-15:30) whose close crosses the level. A bar closing at 15:30
or later lets the session end without a fill. Seven deciders are each
applied to buys and to sells separately, giving 14 candidates.

| ID | Decider | Buys | Sells |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D0 ALWAYS** | The multi-day level, always | Rest at C_t·e^(−σ). Fill at the first bar close ≤ the level in t+1..t+5, else the close of t+5 | Rest at C_t·e^(+σ), mirrored |
| **D1 NEWS AND STRESS** | D0 only where the literature expects a reversal; otherwise the control | Wait (D0) only when the VIX is under 25 at t and there was no earnings release in the last 3 sessions: stress and news moves continue, so those buys act at once | Wait (D0) unless there was an earnings release in the last 3 sessions: news-driven declines continue, while stressed and no-news declines rebound |
| **D2 TURN** | The operator's structural turn | If C_t < EMA10_t (a pullback): fill at the first session close in t+1..t+5 above the prior session's high, else the close of t+5. Otherwise the control | If C_t > EMA10_t: fill at the first close below the prior session's low, else the close of t+5. Otherwise the control |
| **D3 SQUEEZE** | Operator-requested. A squeeze is a 20-day Bollinger bandwidth (4σ₂₀/SMA20) in the lowest tenth of its last 126 sessions | Squeezed: fill at the first session close above the upper band (the breakout), else the close of t+5. Otherwise the control | Squeezed: fill at the first close below the lower band, else the close of t+5. Otherwise the control |
| **D4 M1** | LightGBM on the timing target (below) | Wait with D0's level when ŷ_buy > 0, else the control | Wait with D0's level when ŷ_sell > 0, else the control |
| **D5 M2** | The Jiang-Kelly-Xiu I20 chart CNN on the timing target | as D4 | as D4 |
| **D6 M3** | The stage-3 sequence model (15-minute bars, 60 sessions) on the timing target | as D4 | as D4 |

More on the deciders:

- **D0 is the operator's "wait for the dip" in its plainest multi-day
  form.** It generalizes the board's 1% same-day dip to a one-sigma level
  held for five sessions.
- **D1 is the literature's best-supported condition, applied to each
  side.**
  - Moves with news continue, and moves without news revert
    (Chan 2003, Savor 2012, Da-Liu-Schaumburg 2014).
  - After market-wide stress prices rebound (Nagel 2012).
  - So a buy waits only in calm, no-news conditions. A sell acts at once
    after news, and otherwise waits for the rebound.
  - Earnings days come from `stage3_export.earnings_days` (EDGAR, strict
    publication). The VIX comes from the macro store.
- **D2 is the operator's structural turn in its simplest rule form:** a
  pullback that closes above yesterday's high has turned.
- **D3** tests whether the squeeze's direction, once it breaks, times the
  order.
- **How the memos' recommendations map here.**
  - The structure memo's C1 (delay buys of falling names except in stress,
    buy on the first close above the prior high) is D2 plus D1's stress
    split.
  - Its C5 (hold a downgraded name while it is still rising) is D2's sell
    side.
  - The reversion memo's T2 (a no-news residual dip within five sessions)
    is D1 with D0's level.
  - Their squeeze and RSI(2) controls are represented by D3. RSI(2) is not
    registered: the memo expects it to fail, and it adds a trial without a
    new question.
- **D4-D6 are the question he asked.** Each model sees the grade and every
  input, which is "grade-weighted". It is applied only to the grade's own
  orders, which is "grade-gated". It decides whether to wait, by
  forecasting the gain of waiting.

## Rows and the timing target (models)

- **Rows.** Every T-S1 row of the stage-3 export: 88,063 (name, decision
  date t) pairs of graded members, 2016-2026. The export is
  `stage3_s1.npz` `7fa24f7e`, unchanged. Every grade is included, not only
  the orders. The model learns how each grade's price paths behave, and
  the orders are a subset of these rows.
- **Labels.** Two per row, from the SIP cubes of sessions t+1..t+5:
  - `g_buy(i, t)`: D0's buy fill against the control's buy fill, as g
    above;
  - `g_sell(i, t)`: D0's sell fill against the control's sell fill.

  A row whose five sessions are not all complete cube sessions has NaN
  labels: it is predicted, never trained on.
- **Inputs.** Stage 3's, unchanged:
  - M1 reads the 208 daily columns (the grade one-hot, grade age and
    flips among them);
  - M2 reads the I20 image;
  - M3 reads the 60-session tensor and the daily branch.

  No input is added. The premise under test is the target, not the inputs,
  and stage 3's inputs already hold every technical the operator named.

## Models

Stage 3's registered settings hold except where stated. One model is
trained per family per side, buy and sell, on its own label.

- **M1 LightGBM.**
  - Grid, fixed settings, early stopping and five seeds as stage-3 M1.
  - Objective L2 on the label, winsorized at the fit rows' 0.5% and 99.5%
    quantiles.
  - Bagging fraction 0.7.
  - Selection by pooled Spearman on the validation block (T-I's metric).
  - Refit every 63 sessions.
- **M2, the JKX I20 CNN.** The stage-3 architecture, image, Adam at 1e-5,
  batch 128, patience 2 and five networks.
  - **Deviation (stated).** The two-class head is replaced by one
    regression output. The loss is MSE on the label, winsorized at the fit
    rows' 0.5% and 99.5% quantiles and divided by their standard deviation.
    The question is how much waiting gains, not only whether it gains, so a
    sign classifier would discard the magnitude that decides the trade.
  - Refit every 252 sessions. There is no grid.
- **M3, the sequence model.** The stage-3 T-S1 architecture: 60 sessions
  of 27 steps, attention pooling and the daily branch.
  - The target is standardized and winsorized as T-I's.
  - The grid is `SEQ_GRID`; the fixed settings are `SEQ_FIXED`.
  - Five seeds of the chosen configuration.
  - Refit every 252 sessions.
- **The walk-forward.** It is `stage3_io.folds`:
  - the first test block at session index 500;
  - validation on the last 252 sessions of each training window;
  - **gap 11**: a label reach of 6 sessions (t+1..t+5, then the fill) plus
    a 5-session embargo, at both the fit/validation and the
    training/test boundaries;
  - deterministic runs and saved per-seed forecasts.

## The decision test

- **Orders.** The control executor is run at 20 offsets (the reset
  phases) over the panel, as stage 3's T-S1 ran it. A journal records every
  decision's submitted units with its kind: reset, top-up, redeploy,
  mid-cycle or rotation. Each changed position is one order, with its
  side, kind, grade at t and weight.
- **Pricing.** Each candidate re-prices every order from the SIP cubes,
  against the control on the same order.
  - An order whose sessions lack a complete cube fills as the control, and
    is counted.
  - An order a model has no forecast for fills as the control. This covers
    the orders before the model's first test block.
- **Windows.**
  - **The model window** runs from the latest first forecast date among M1
    to M3 through 2023-12-29. It holds for all 14 candidates, so rules and
    models are judged on the same orders.
  - **2024-2026** is the later window.
- **Statistics.** At the median offset:
  - the session series of book gain, with a Newey-West t at lag 20;
  - the gain per re-timed order (orders whose fill differs from the
    control's), with the t clustered by date.
- **Robustness: the next-bar run.** Every fill at a bar close moves to the
  next bar's open. A fill at the official close stays there. This applies
  to candidate and control alike.
- **Reported, never promoting:**
  - results by side, kind and grade group: A+ vs A buys; exits to B vs to
    C; reset trims vs rotation exits;
  - results by VIX regime;
  - the share of orders that waited, and how long;
  - the oracle on the same orders (the best bar close within five
    sessions) and the share of it each candidate captures;
  - each model's population skill by grade: pooled Spearman, and the
    decision's hit rate.

## Kill criteria, fixed now

A candidate (a decider on one side) **REPLACES** the board's convention
for that side only if all of these hold:

1. On the model window it gains at least **+2.0 bp a session with t ≥
   2.0** at the median offset.
2. It holds at the same floor in the next-bar run.
3. It is **not negative on 2024-2026**.
4. It clears a **deflated Sharpe ratio ≥ 0.95 at N = 14**
   (`candidate_stats.deflated_sharpe`), with the across-candidate variance
   of the 14 candidates' excess.
5. For models, it is **seed-stable**: fewer than 2 sign changes in the 5
   single-seed runs.
6. **It is timing, not exposure.** The drift-adjusted gain must also clear
   **+1.0 bp a session with t ≥ 2.0** on the model window.
   - Per order, the drift-adjusted gain is g − s · μ · d:
     - d is the sessions the order waited;
     - μ is the mean daily log return of the book's A/A+ names over the
       same window;
     - s is +1 for a sell (waiting holds the stock) and −1 for a buy
       (waiting holds cash).
   - A sell delay that only rides the market's rise fails here. A buy that
     clears the floor has already beaten the drift.

Anything else is **RECORD**. A candidate that fails the floor but gains at
least 25 bp per re-timed order, with a date-clustered t ≥ 3, is **RECORD:
real but immaterial**.

A REPLACE changes nothing by itself. The live path stays frozen at
`checkpoint-2026-09-28`. A pass becomes a separate, registered live change
only with the operator's go-ahead, and its board wording keeps his rule:
one word and a size.

  - M2: 1 × 2 = 2.

  It is disclosed above because it shaped W and D1.

## Prior, stated before the run

- **The literature memos.** They put about 3-8% on any multi-day timing
  rule adding +2 bp a session here. The obstacle is arithmetic as much as
  signal:
  - the book places about 0.4-0.8 orders a session at 5-9% of equity;
  - +2 bp a session needs roughly 40-60 bp gained on every order;
  - the diagnostic's oracle is about 230-290 bp, so a candidate must
    capture about a fifth of perfect foresight.
- **This note's prior:**

  | Outcome | Prior |
  |---|---|
  | Any REPLACE | about 5% |
  | A "real but immaterial" reading | about 15% |
  | A sell-side candidate doing better than a buy-side one on the raw gain | 60%. Waiting to sell holds the market's drift, and a higher close comes 81% of the time. Criterion 6 decides whether it is timing |
  | A model's population Spearman on the timing target | 0.02-0.06. Stage 1's next-day rank IC was 0.011-0.013, and five-day paths carry more structure |

**What would make the prior wrong:** a model whose waits concentrate
where the diagnostic shows little drift (calm markets, no news) and whose
population skill holds in 2024-2026.
