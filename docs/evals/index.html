A screening test correctly identifies 95% of people who have a condition, and correctly clears 95% of people who do not. The condition affects about 1 person in 500. Someone tests positive. How likely is it that they actually have the condition?

deepseek-v4-flash

Given: - Sensitivity (true positive rate) = 95% → if someone has the condition, test is positive 95% of the time. - Specificity (true negative rate) = 95% → if someone does **not** have the condition, test is negative 95% of the time. - Prevalence = 1 in 500 = 0.2% A positive test result: - Probability of a true positive: 0.95 × 0.002 = 0.0019 - Probability of a false positive: 0.05 × 0.998 = 0.0499 - Total probability of testing positive: 0.0019 + 0.0499 = 0.0518 So the chance they actually have the condition is: 0.0019 / 0.0518 ≈ 0.0367, or about **3.7%**. So even with a positive screening result, the probability they truly have the condition is only about **3.7%** — because the condition is rare and most positive results are false positives.
